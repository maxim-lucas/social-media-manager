Copy blocks

What reach is actually made ofThe five things Instagram counts

Adam Mosseri has been unusually direct about this in 2026. In rough order of weight for a Reel:

  • 1Sends One person forwarding your Reel in a DM is worth far more than a like. Instagram reads it as "this was worth interrupting a friend for." This is the metric to design posts around.
  • 2Watch time & completion Explore and the Reels tab weight the probability someone watches 95%+ of the video. A 59-second Reel that holds 30 seconds beats a 15-second one that holds 6.
  • 3Saves Strong on carousels specifically — a how-to people intend to come back to. Your 12-slide tutorial is built for this.
  • 4Likes and comments Still counted, but the weakest of the four. A comment you reply to is worth more than one you don't.
  • 5Originality Content that looks recycled from another platform gets down-ranked. Never upload a version with a TikTok watermark — export clean from the source file every time.
The uncomfortable part

Hashtags are not on this list. In July 2026 Mosseri said plainly that hashtags "work, but they've never been a good way to actually increase your reach" — they help "ever so slightly on the margins." Use a few because they classify your post and feed search. Do not build a strategy on them. There's a set further down; it takes ten seconds to apply and then you stop thinking about it.

Do this before you post anythingSet the profile up so it converts

The profile is the conversion step. Someone watches the Reel, taps your name, and decides in about three seconds whether to install. Right now that page has to answer one question: what does this do for me and is it free?

Account type and settings

  • Professional → Business, not Creator. Business gives you the contact button, Meta Business Suite scheduling, and eligibility to run ads on the same posts later.
  • Public. A private account is invisible to recommendations.
  • Settings → "Similar account suggestions" ON. This puts you in the suggested-accounts rail after someone follows a comparable account. It's free distribution and it's off by default for some accounts.
  • Sharing → allow Remixes and sharing to Stories. Every barrier you leave up is a send you don't get.
  • Turn on auto-generated captions for Reels. A large share of viewers watch muted — and yours is currently silent in French anyway.

The Name field is the one that gets searched

Instagram's in-app search indexes your username and the Name field far more strongly than the bio. The Name field is 30 characters and most brands waste it by repeating the handle. Put a keyword in it.

Links, category, highlights

  • Instagram allows up to five links. Use three: App Store, Google Play, and your site. Label them clearly — "Download for iPhone", "Download for Android", "How it works". This beats a link-in-bio service, which adds a tap and loses people.
  • Category: Shopping & Retail. Add the email contact button.
  • Four Story highlights, in this order, using your 9:16 frames as covers: How it works Real claims Stores FAQ

Publishing the ReelThe checklist that matters

  • Cover frame: use the receipt shot around 0:05 — the one where the total reads $3,340.71. A number on the cover earns the tap.
  • Your first three seconds are doing the work. The film opens on the receipt printing and the total landing, which is the right instinct. If a Reel underperforms twice, re-cut the first three seconds before you change anything else.
  • Length: 59.4 s is in the right band. Keep future cuts under 90 seconds.
  • Audio: the English cut has the voice-over. For the French cut, either wait for the regenerated VO or add a trending audio track inside Instagram at post time — picking in-app audio helps reach more than a voice-over does, and the on-screen text carries the whole story regardless.
  • Never post an export that carries another platform's watermark. Upload the source file to each platform separately.
  • Post it to Feed as well as Reels (the toggle in the sharing screen). Two surfaces, one upload.
Trial Reels — not yet

Trial Reels show a video to non-followers only for 72 hours before you decide whether to publish it to followers. It's the best testing tool Instagram has ever shipped, and it needs a public professional account with 1,000+ followers. Put it on the list for when you cross that line — then use it to test hooks against the same body of the video.

Publishing the carouselBuilt to be saved

Use the 4:5 set (1080×1350) — it's the tallest ratio the feed allows, so it occupies the most screen. The 9:16 set is for Stories and standalone posts. Instagram allows up to 20 slides; twelve is the right length for a tutorial.

A carousel earns reach differently from a Reel: every slide someone swipes to is dwell time, and a genuinely useful sequence gets saved. Slide 12 is your call to action, so people who swipe all the way land on the download card.

Timing: post when your own audience is on, which Insights will tell you once you have a few hundred followers. Until then, weekday early evening Eastern (6–8 pm) is the reasonable default for a Canadian consumer app — and consistency of time matters more than the specific hour.

One account or twoBilingual, and Quebec specifically

Start with one account. Splitting a new account in two halves your follower count, your engagement rate and your posting cadence on both — and engagement rate is what the recommendation system reads. Post English and French as separate posts on the same account, each with a single-language caption. Don't stack both languages in one caption; it doubles the length and halves the read rate.

Split into @priceback and @priceback.qc once one language reliably outperforms the other and you have enough volume to feed both. That's a good problem, not a launch problem.

Worth knowing

Quebec engagement with French content runs dramatically higher than with English, and Quebec's Charter of the French Language sets expectations for commercial communication in the province. Genuinely localised French — which is what you have, right down to 2 799,99 $ rather than $2,799.99 — is doing real work here. Worth having a Quebec-based reader check the copy before you scale spend.

Reading the numbersWhat to look at, what to ignore

Instagram Insights will give you a dozen metrics. Four of them tell you something you can act on:

  • Sends per view. The one to optimise. Compare each post to your own median rather than to a published benchmark — the absolute number matters far less than the trend across your posts.
  • Views from non-followers. This is the recommendation system deciding whether to push you. A post that's 80% followers didn't get picked up; a post that's 80% non-followers did.
  • Average watch time. Where in the video people leave. If there's a cliff, it's almost always in the first five seconds.
  • Profile visits → link taps. Your actual funnel. If Reels get views but nobody taps through, the problem is the profile, not the video.

Ignore follower count for the first month. It's the slowest-moving number on the page and it will make you change things that were working.

Two things to get rightBefore you put money behind it

Using the Costco name

Naming Costco to describe what the app does is ordinary descriptive use, and the film already avoids their logo, their wordmark styling, and any implied endorsement — the TV is deliberately unbranded for the same reason. Keep it that way: no Costco logo, no styling that reads as an official Costco product, no language suggesting a partnership. Paid Meta ads get reviewed against a stricter bar than organic posts, so have someone qualified look at the ad creative before you spend.

Don't promise the refund

The policy has conditions — the item has to be in stock at the lower price, inside the window. The film states this in the fine print on the claim frame, and the captions above say "claim" rather than "get". Keep that discipline in every caption. One screenshot of an over-promise is worse than a hundred posts of accurate ones.
